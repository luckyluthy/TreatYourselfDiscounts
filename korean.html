🇰🇷 TYD's Korean Corner

Learning to read Korean with colors, one block at a time. This is TYD's own study corner at the Pond.

How TYD learns it

Hangul letters stack into little blocks. Each block is read in order: the first consonant, then the vowel, then a final consonant (called batchim) if there is one. TYD studies with a color coded method, where consonants, vowels, and batchim each get their own color, so a block shows its pieces at a glance.

A favorite example: 사랑 (sarang) means love. 사 is s + a, and 랑 is r + a + ng batchim.

Where the vowel sits

Tall vowels stand on the side of the first consonant, like 여자 (woman). Wide vowels sit on the bottom, like 우유 (milk). Once you know which kind a vowel is, every block you meet is built the same way.
